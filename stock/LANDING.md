## The problem (one line, then the twist)

Run out of buns at lunch and you lose the sale. Over-order and it rots in the walk-in. Every store
manager is running this trade-off in their head, twice a day, across 2,000 kitchens — from memory,
under a lunch rush.

Scott runs it from the numbers. Every count, receipt, recipe, and waste log — reconciled, not
remembered.

---

## What Scott does (the four duties — this is the whole job)

**Variance & root cause.** Scott reconciles what stock *should* remain against what was counted —
opening + received − sold-by-recipe − waste — and names the gap: over-portioning, spoilage, theft,
a miscount, a short delivery. From the evidence, not a hunch. When the counts reconcile, it says so
and moves on.

**Demand forecast.** From six months of sales, weekday by weekday, per store, per SKU. It knows
Fridays aren't Tuesdays and a 400-head catering order on Thursday isn't noise — it pre-positions for
it. Graded against what actually sold, so it aims for the honest number, never the padded one.

**Smart reorder.** Turns the forecast into an order you can actually place — whole cases, never a
convenient half-pack, and never more perishable stock than sells before it expires. Lettuce doesn't
keep a week, and Scott knows it.

**Waste markdown.** Spots the lot that won't clear before its date and prices it to move — *today,
not at the deadline*. It'll show you the trade-off out loud: 20% off now clears it, 10% off tomorrow
nets more if sell-through holds. Then it picks the one that recovers the most.

## Why Scott is different

**It shows its work.** Push back on any call and Scott justifies it from its actual trace — the
query it ran, the constraint it applied, the number it landed on. Not a canned line. A well-documented
answer, itemized down to the row it came from.

**It knows when to say nothing.** Most inventory tools cry wolf to look useful. Scott reports "counts
reconcile within tolerance, forecast within 3%" and leaves it there. A quiet week is a good week.

**It stays in its lane.** Ask Scott about a customer refund or the company's books and it declines —
that's not inventory. It won't guess, won't fabricate a number, and won't answer a question the data
can't support. It would rather say "I can't tell from this" than be confidently wrong.

**It doesn't work in a silo.** When a store's about to run short and a neighbor's overstocked, Scott
recommends the transfer instead of an emergency order — and flags the front line that a shortfall may
hit tonight, before the complaints do. One root cause, coordinated actions, no human stitching it
together.

- **Bottom line.** For a business that reports to the street as MCTX, food cost is the lever. Scott

## How it works (for the technical buyer)

Scott is a Claude Managed Agent — a model, a system prompt, a set of skills, and the tools it's wired
to. It reads your inventory the way an analyst would: it investigates before it answers, computes in
SQL so the numbers are grounded by construction, and records every decision through an auditable
action. Nothing is stored, guessed, or memorized. It's inventory reconciliation you can read
end-to-end.
