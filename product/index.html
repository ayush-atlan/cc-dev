The trade-off every manager runs from memory

Run out of buns at lunch and you lose the sale. Over-order and it rots in the walk-in. Every store manager runs this twice a day, across 2,000 kitchens — from memory, under a lunch rush. Scott runs it from the numbers. Every count, receipt, recipe, and waste log — reconciled, not remembered.

The whole job, four ways

Variance & root cause

Reconciles what stock should remain against what was counted — opening + received − sold-by-recipe − waste — and names the gap: over-portioning, spoilage, theft, a miscount, a short delivery. From the evidence.

Demand forecast

Six months of sales, weekday by weekday, per store, per SKU. Fridays aren't Tuesdays, and a 400-head catering order isn't noise — it pre-positions. Graded against what actually sold.

Smart reorder

Turns the forecast into an order you can place — whole cases, never a convenient half-pack, never more perishable stock than sells before it expires. Lettuce doesn't keep a week, and Scott knows it.

Waste markdown

Spots the lot that won't clear before its date and prices it to move — today, not at the deadline. Shows the trade-off out loud, then picks the discount that recovers the most.

A real catch, itemized

Asked to reconcile cheese at store str_001 for the week of Jun 17–23, Scott ran one query: opening 60 + received 25 − recipe-implied usage 51.47 − waste 0.28 = expected 33.25 vs counted 25.25 → an 8-unit gap. Waste didn't explain it. Scott read the shift log, found a Jun 21 note — a new griddle hand over-portioning cheese on Big Schema builds, "not weighed off" — and filed it as over_portion. That's $43.20 of shrink surfaced with a root cause, not a shrug. (Live result on the read-only ledger, cheese at $5.40/kg.)

A quiet week is a good week. Most inventory tools cry wolf to look useful. Asked about near-expiry cheese at the same store, Scott checked, found the only lot 10 days out, and said "no markdown needed." It reports "counts reconcile within tolerance" and leaves it there.

Why Scott is different

It shows its work. Push back on any call and Scott justifies it from its actual trace — the query it ran, the constraint it applied, the number it landed on. Itemized down to the row it came from.

It stays in its lane. Ask about a customer refund or the company's books and it declines — that's not inventory. It won't guess, won't fabricate a number, and would rather say "I can't tell from this" than be confidently wrong.

It doesn't work in a silo. When a store's about to run short and a neighbor's overstocked, Scott recommends the transfer instead of an emergency order — and flags the front line before the complaints do. (Rebalancing & hand-off shown for the pitch; the graded agent submits the four duties.)
