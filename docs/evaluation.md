# How you're judged

You're an agency selling a solution to McContext. Three things decide how you do.

## 1. The bench - does the agent actually work

The platform runs your registered agent against real, **hidden** cases. Depending on the challenge,
scoring is by **exact rules** (we planted the problems, so we know every right answer; points =
what you caught, minus false alarms) and/or an **AI judge** (did it reason and explain itself
clearly and correctly). The bench is **one input**, not the whole grade.

## 2. The product - did you build something sellable

You're not just shipping an agent, you're pitching a product. A UI, a company website, a SaaS
walkthrough video, a deck - whatever makes the case. Quality and efficiency matter; a thing that
works *and* sells beats a clever script. You can deploy your product (e.g. on Railway) or show it
locally to the organizers.

## 3. The pitch - convince the people who'd hire you

Organizers from McContext weigh your build, your bench results, and your pitch. The **top 10 teams
present live to McContext's founders, Varun and Prukalpa**, with their deck, product, and
walkthrough.

## Reliability matters

The bench runs your agent on many cases, not one. A right answer you can't reproduce isn't worth
much - build for the agent that's right *every* time.

## What loses points

- **Making things up** - inventing policy, prices, or numbers the data doesn't support.
- **False alarms** - flagging what isn't actually wrong.
- **Over-reach** - acting beyond what's warranted.

Each challenge's `task.md` lists its specifics.
